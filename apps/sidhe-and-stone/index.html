## What it is A cozy management sim about the unglamorous half of archaeology. You have a patch of Irish ground, a wheelbarrow, and a staff you can't quite afford. The mounds are full of things that were buried on purpose, and the modern museum is full of things that were buried by accident. Both are your problem. Everything is pixel art. Every sprite is drawn on a 16×16 or 24×24 grid in a single shared palette, so the interface and the artwork cannot drift apart.

A day in the life

  1. Pick a dig. Test pits are cheap and shallow. Shafts and deepworks cost real money and can cost you a site.
  2. Work the site. Deeper digs take longer and find better things. The day's work feeds the reel.
  3. Spin the reel. A slot machine of clovers, honey, moons, stars, bog and blight. A miss is never a loss — it just pays the day.
  4. Curate and exhibit. Raw finds are worthless until a curator grades them. What goes on the floor is what visitors actually see.
  5. End the day. Takings come in. Staff get paid. Sometimes something happens that you did not plan for.

A real economy

There's a headless simulator behind the game that plays thousands of days without a human. It checks that income stays stable, that every cash milestone is worth chasing, and that the random event deck doesn't quietly tilt the whole campaign.

Six grades of find

Rubble, clay, tablet, relic, mask, crown. Each grade has its own colour, its own shelf, and its own appetite for curators.

Guests with opinions

Visitors arrive, look at what's on show, and leave a number behind. Put your best artefacts on the floor or don't bother.

A gallery you fill

Four pedestals, and only you decide what stands on them. The rest waits in the vault, which is where good museums hide the things they haven't explained yet.

Two builds that agree

The native version and the browser version run the same simulation, and a parity suite checks they produce identical results — day for day, coin for coin.

Plays on your phone

The browser build is designed for a thumb first: big targets, one-handed reach, and a reel you can actually hit.

## Download for Mac There's a native macOS build — a real app bundle, universal (Apple silicon and Intel), with its own icon. It's a direct download, no account needed: **[Download SidheAndStone.dmg](https://github.com/TheSolai/sidhe-and-stone/releases/download/v0.2.0/SidheAndStone.dmg)** Drag the app out of the disk image and drop it in your Applications folder. That's the whole installation.

One honest caveat

The Mac build is signed ad-hoc, not with an Apple Developer certificate, and it isn't notarised. It runs fine on your own machine. On someone else's Mac, Gatekeeper will likely refuse to open it until they right-click → Open, or you move it with xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine. A properly signed and notarised build needs a paid Apple Developer account. That's a purchase, not a setting.

## How it's made Godot 4 for the native build, TypeScript and Canvas for the browser build, and one shared palette that both read from. The sprite files are the source of truth; the PNGs are generated from them. An automated check verifies every sprite: right grid size, right colour count, a single opaque region, lit from the top-left, and zero pixels that aren't in the palette. Everything about the game is in the [repository](https://github.com/TheSolai/sidhe-and-stone) — the design documents, the economy simulator, the test suites, and the build scripts.
